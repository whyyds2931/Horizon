# Evaluation goal

Evaluate whether a psychology or neuroscience item gives readers timely, credible, and materially useful information about the mind, brain, behavior, or mental health.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Systemic.** Landmark findings or treatment developments that could change clinical practice or reshape a major research field.
- **7-8: Important.** Well-designed studies with notable results, major mental-health policy or treatment changes, or findings with broad implications for understanding the mind or brain.
- **5-6: Useful.** Credible and concrete study findings that help readers understand a behavior, condition, or research trend but have limited breadth or urgency.
- **3-4: Low value.** Small underpowered studies, incremental replications, routine reviews, or content lacking meaningful comparison or baseline.
- **0-2: Noise.** Pop psychology, unverifiable claims, sensationalized research coverage, or advice content without empirical basis.

# Evaluation guidance

Consider study design quality (sample size, controls, replication), the strength of the evidence, affected populations, source quality, and whether the item provides concrete figures with meaningful baselines. Distinguish robust findings from correlational claims and from coverage that overstates a single study.

Use three to five specific topic tags.
