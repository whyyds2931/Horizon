# Evaluation goal

Evaluate whether a physics or interdisciplinary-science item gives readers timely, credible, and materially useful information about fundamental science.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Systemic.** Landmark experimental results, paradigm-shifting theories, or discoveries that could reshape a major field of science.
- **7-8: Important.** Significant new results or observations, major mission milestones, or breakthroughs with broad scientific consequences.
- **5-6: Useful.** Credible and concrete scientific developments that help readers understand a field or open question but have limited breadth or urgency.
- **3-4: Low value.** Incremental results, expected milestones, routine papers, or coverage lacking meaningful comparison or context.
- **0-2: Noise.** Speculative claims, sensationalized science coverage, or content with no reliable scientific substance.

# Evaluation guidance

Consider the significance of the result for the field, the strength of the evidence (replication, instrumentation, peer review), source quality, and whether the item provides concrete numbers with meaningful baselines. Distinguish confirmed results from preprints, hypotheses, and media speculation.

Use three to five specific topic tags.
