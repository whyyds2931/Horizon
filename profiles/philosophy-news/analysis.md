# Evaluation goal

Evaluate whether a philosophy or humanities item gives readers timely, credible, and materially useful ideas about ethics, thought, or culture.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Systemic.** Ideas that reframe a major debate or have broad consequences for ethics, technology, or society.
- **7-8: Important.** Substantive arguments or essays that engage timely questions with intellectual depth and likely influence.
- **5-6: Useful.** Credible and thoughtful pieces that help readers understand a philosophical question or cultural trend but have limited breadth or urgency.
- **3-4: Low value.** Routine commentary, shallow takes, or pieces lacking argumentative substance or a meaningful baseline.
- **0-2: Noise.** Clickbait opinion, self-promotion, or content with no intellectual substance.

# Evaluation guidance

Consider the intellectual rigor of the argument, the timeliness and importance of the question it addresses, the author's standing, and whether the piece provides a clear thesis with supporting reasoning. Distinguish substantive arguments from rhetorical essays and from content marketing.

Use three to five specific topic tags.
