# Evaluation goal

Evaluate whether a biotech or life-sciences item gives readers timely, credible, and materially useful information about biology, medicine, or the life-science industry.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Systemic.** Landmark regulatory decisions, first-in-class approvals, or discoveries that reshape a disease area or major field of research.
- **7-8: Important.** Significant clinical trial results, major approvals, notable mechanism discoveries, or breakthroughs with broad scientific or clinical consequences.
- **5-6: Useful.** Credible and concrete developments that help readers understand a disease, technology, or research trend but have limited breadth or urgency.
- **3-4: Low value.** Routine incremental findings, expected study results, small financings, or lab announcements with no meaningful comparison or baseline.
- **0-2: Noise.** Unverified health claims, promotional biotech content, or sensational research reporting.

# Evaluation guidance

Consider the strength of the scientific evidence (trial phase, sample size, peer review), the breadth of patients or fields affected, source quality, and whether the item provides concrete figures with meaningful baselines. Distinguish reported findings from company announcements and from speculative science journalism.

Use three to five specific topic tags.
